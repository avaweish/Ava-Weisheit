Why the Caitlin Clark Effect Is Bigger Than One Player

Sports Analytics Data Science

I'll admit it - I picked this topic because I'm a Caitlin Clark fan. But once I actually sat down with the attendance numbers for my final project in INFO-I369, I realized there was a more interesting story here than "she's good, so people show up." The "Clark effect" headlines were doing a lot more narrative lifting than the data could actually back up on its own.

The stat everyone quotes

League-wide attendance did jump during her rookie season, and games she played in consistently drew bigger crowds, home or away. That part checks out. What surprised me was how concentrated that lift actually was - a handful of markets and marquee matchups were doing most of the work, not some even, league-wide bump like the headlines implied.

What the regression actually said

Once I ran a linear regression against attendance and Google Trends data, Clark's presence came back as a statistically significant predictor - but far from the only one. Win percentage, rivalry games, and where a team sat in the season schedule all mattered too. Pulling her individual effect out from all of that meant controlling for those other variables, which is exactly the unglamorous work that gets skipped when someone's writing a headline instead of running a model.

Why I keep thinking about this

This ended up being less a basketball project and more a lesson I now see everywhere in sports analytics: one compelling storyline - a star player, a big trade, a viral clip - becomes the explanation for something that's actually driven by five overlapping factors. That doesn't mean the storyline is wrong. Clark's impact is real, I saw it in the numbers. It just means being honest about how much of the effect is really hers versus everything else happening around her at the same time.
